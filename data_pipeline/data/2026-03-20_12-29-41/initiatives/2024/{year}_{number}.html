PsychedeliCare

Information from the European Commission

11/09/2024

Commission Decision on the registration of the citizens' initiative

Information from the organisers

Disclaimer

The texts below are the sole responsibility of the organisers of the initiative. They reflect solely the views of their authors and can in no way be taken to reflect the views of the European Commission.

Objectives

We call on the European Commission to foster equitable, timely, affordable, safe, and legal access to innovative psychedelic-assisted therapies.


  1. The Commission should support the establishment of an expert consensus on standards of psychedelic care, with psychological support, therapist training, ethical guidelines and safety measures, to ensure the safe and effective rollout of psychedelic therapies. The Commission should back capacity building efforts for multidisciplinary training programs on mental health, specifically designed for psychedelic therapy training for healthcare providers.
  2. The Commission should boost EU-funded research into the therapeutic applications of psychedelics to strengthen the evidence of their safety and efficacy. It should support the development of research networks focusing on innovative therapies.
  3. The Commission should adopt common positions within UN fora to advocate for pragmatic, progressive transnational regulations concerning psychedelic compounds, and make appropriate recommendations for the rescheduling of psychedelic compounds in the 1971 UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances.

Annex

There is a mental health crisis in the EU.


One in six people suffer from mental health conditions, and half felt depressed or anxious in the last 12 months as of June 2023. OECD data shows the economic costs of mental illnesses have been estimated at 4.2% of the Union’s annual GDP, equating to €600 billion a year.


Up to 50% of patients do not respond to the psychiatric treatments currently available. There have been no substantial innovations in the field of mental health since the advent of SSRIs, which are the most commonly prescribed antidepressants. The EU has approved only one new psychiatric treatment in the last three years, in stark contrast to the 68 approvals in oncology. This trend persists despite the escalating rates of anxiety, depression, and persistent feelings of sadness or hopelessness that have become increasingly prevalent in our population.


However, a growing body of scientific evidence indicates the effectiveness of psychedelic-assisted therapies . These patient-centric personalised treatment pathways can provide safe, rapid and robust clinical improvements with durable effects in treating conditions such as post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, end-of-life anxiety, and substance use disorders.


  1. In the US, the FDA has granted five “ breakthrough therapy ” designations for MDMA, psilocybin, and LSD treatments.
  2. In Canada, psychedelic-assisted therapy has been permitted since the beginning of 2022 for treating patients suffering from end-of-life anxiety, due to a life-threatening condition resistant to other treatments.
  3. Switzerland also allowed such therapies for specific cases and contributed to research.
  4. In the UK, Europe’s first commercial facility for psychedelic drug trials has opened in London, and the Centre for Psychedelic Research at the Imperial College London is focusing on the use of psychedelics in mental health care.
  5. In Australia, psychiatrists are authorised to prescribe MDMA for addressing PTSD, and psilocybin for treating treatment-resistant depression.


In the European Union, scientific research is insufficient and highly dependent on Member States. At the same time, mental health has moved up the European agenda, particularly since the COVID-19 crisis. We recognise the efforts undertaken by the Commission through its actions stemming from the "Communication on a comprehensive approach to mental health", and hope that the flagship programmes included could address the capacity building needs for training the healthcare on the standards of care for psychedelic therapies.


In the context of a global psychedelic renaissance, the Commission should build on this momentum to proactively establish the legal framework and medical conditions for integrating psychedelics into therapy in the EU. Access to innovative mental health therapies should be part of the European Commission’s priority actions on mental health. Multidisciplinary training programmes on mental health for health professionals, modelled on the EU-PROMENS project, could help train thousands of medical professionals in the best practices and standards of care for psychedelic therapies, striving towards the equal accessibility to these innovative treatments for patients in the EU.


As State parties to the International Covenant on Economic Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR), all EU member states are duty-bound to respect, protect and fulfil the Right to the Highest Attainable Standard of Health , as set out in Article 12 as well as the freedom of research and the right to enjoy the benefits of scientific progress and its application , as set out in Article 15 of the same Covenant.


We propose the Article 168 of the TFEU as the legal basis of this initiative, as it states that the Union action “ shall be directed towards improving public health, preventing physical and mental illness and diseases ”, and it empowers the Council and the Parliament, acting in accordance with the ordinary legislative procedure, to adopt “ measures setting high standards of quality and safety for medicinal products and devices for medical use .”


We call on the Commission to recognise the unique specificities of psychedelic therapies and their potential to meet high unmet needs for mental health conditions and substance use disorders, which, even if not immediately life-threatening, impose a burden on individuals, healthcare systems, societies, and economies. Special regulatory frameworks or sandboxes, which allow experimenting with new regulatory approaches for novel therapies in a limited part of a sector or area, could help develop and evaluate new care standards for optimal, personalised treatment pathways, and address uncertainties in clinical settings, therapy combinations, and patient unmet needs.


We urge the European Commission to foster equitable, timely, affordable, safe, and legal access to innovative psychedelic-assisted therapies. The following strategies could be implemented:


  1. Establishing research partnerships and joint actions between the EU and Member States,
  2. Offering incentives such as increased regulatory protection, market entry rewards and milestone payment reward schemes,
  3. Providing enhanced EMA support including through PRIME,
  4. Creating a European Hub for Mental Health R&D, or a European Research Network,
  5. Implementing accelerated assessments, conditional market approvals, and adaptive pathways,
  6. Ensuring a more predictable reimbursement environment.
